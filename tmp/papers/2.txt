A Hybrid Kinematic GCN and Elastic Transformer for Efficient
Indian Sign Language Recognition
Pushp Garg·Himangshu Sarma·Mrinmoy Ghorai
Abstract Sign Language Recognition (SLR) presents
complex challenges due to variable signing speeds and
spatial intricacies. Specifically, Indian Sign Language
(ISL) requires precise modeling of its inherently biman-
ual nature and is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-
vocabulary benchmarks and the inability of standard
models to handle irregular signing velocities. To address
this, a structurally-aware hybrid framework for efficient,
gloss ISL recognition is proposed. To minimize compu-
tational redundancy, an entropy-driven SVD sampler
that dynamically extracts informative keyframes is used.
Because this subsampling creates irregular physical time
gaps, the proposed architecture employs a Continuous
Dual Positional Encoding paired with a distance-aware
Elastic Transformer to preserve natural signing rhythm.
Spatially, a Kinematically-Conditioned Adaptive GCN
explicitly models bimanual geometry using unified bone-
joint features. To validate our framework, a 120-class,
9,453-video native ISL dataset is created. Augmented
by Physical Node Dropout and ArcFace optimization to
mitigate subject-specific memorization, our framework
achieves a competitive 89.8% accuracy, establishing a
strong baseline for complex sign language modeling.

signing rhythm, a Continuous Dual Positional Encod-

2 Related W ork
Broader Sign Language ProcessingThe research com-
munity has actively pursued tools and techniques for
processing sign languages, establishing an intersection
between the computer vision [ 31] and natural language
processing [ 62] domains. Consequently, a wide array
of sign language processing (SLP) tasks have been for-
malized. These include sign language detection [ 37],
identification [36], segmentation [6], generation [59,48],
and continuous translation [7,38,63,39,20]. While self-
supervised pretraining [ 49] and phonological modeling
[54] have recently gained traction, the severe unavailabil-
ity of densely annotated resources remains the primary
challenge for robust, data-driven SLP approaches.
ISL Datasets and Recognition ConstraintsData-driven
Sign Language Recognition (SLR) is strictly constrained
by the availability of large-scale benchmarks. Early In-
dian Sign Language (ISL) research predominantly fo-
cused on static alphabet recognition using 2D object
detectors [53] or limited-vocabulary gestures [ 35,42,26].
For dynamic temporal glosses, large-vocabulary cor-
pora (Table 1) like CISLR, introduced by [ 22], and
INCLUDE [50] provided foundational baselines. How-
ever, their severe sparsity (e.g., CISLR averaging 1.5
videos per word) forces models into complex few-shot
or prototypical learning paradigms to compensate for
data scarcity [ 5]. While recent large-scale continuous
benchmarks address sentence-level translation [ 23,21],
a gap remains for deep, isolated gloss evaluation where
high sample density per class allows models to learn
identity-invariant kinematics. Our proposed 120-class
dataset directly addresses this by providing substan-
tial depth (averaging ∼78 videos per class), delivering
the data density necessary to learn identity-invariant
kinematics without few-shot workarounds.
Architectural Modeling for Sign LanguageStandard spa-
tial temporal architectures, including 3D CNNs and stan-
dard Video Transformers, struggle to capture the com-
plex bimanual nature and irregular rhythmic velocities
of ISL, resulting in sub-optimal recognition [ 23,21]. Fur-
thermore, standard temporal graph networks implicitly
assume uniform frame sampling [65]. This assumption is
inherently flawed for sign language: it wastes computa-
tion on static hold-phases while risking the loss of rapid
micro-motions. As demonstrated in our ablation study
(Table 7), replacing entropy-driven SVD sampling with
uniform temporal sampling causes a 2.3% accuracy drop,
directly confirming that content-aware frame selection
is critical. The proposed architecture directly addresses
these established bottlenecks by replacing naive spatial
processing with a Kinematically-Conditioned Adaptive
GCN and bypassing the uniform-sampling assumption
via a distance-aware Elastic Temporal Transformer.
Irregular Temporal ModelingA parallel line of work
in sequence modeling addresses the challenge of non-
uniform temporal spacing. Continuous-time Transform-
ers such as ContiFormer [8] extend standard attention
to handle continuous-time inputs by incorporating time-
delta information into the attention kernel, while Neural
ODE-based approaches model latent dynamics between
irregular observations. Our Continuous Dual Positional
Encoding and distance-aware attention bias are moti-
vated by this paradigm but are specifically tailored for
sign language: rather than modeling continuous-time
dynamics, we encode both the discrete logical ordering
of entropy-selected keyframes and their absolute phys-
ical timestamps, allowing the Elastic Transformer to
reason about irregular physical gaps without sacrificing
sequential coherence.

Instead of uniform processing, the architecture par-

nodes), and Right Hand (21 nodes) each processed by a

To capture the bimanual coordination crucial to sign

three Part-GCNs are concatenated with this interaction
token, constructing a holistic, structurally-aware pose
token.

4 Experiments
This section comprehensively evaluates the efficacy and
efficiency of the proposed framework. A 120-class native
ISL benchmark is introduced. Subsequently, the archi-
tectural contributions are evaluated through rigorous
comparisons against established baselines and public
datasets, concluding with detailed ablation studies and
computational profiling.
4.1 Dataset Description
The scarcity of large-vocabulary skeletal benchmarks
severely bottlenecks dynamic ISL recognition. To pro-
vide a robust testbed, a native 120-class ISL reposi-
tory comprising 9,453 videos (averaging 42.2 frames per
video) is collected. The vocabulary spans 5 semantic
categories (T able 2). Class frequencies range from 33
to 97 videos per gloss; this imbalance necessitates the
dynamic class-weighted loss formulation from Section
3.5.
Acquisition and Partitioning:Data was recorded using
a Kinect V2 at 15 FPS. To maximize real-world we-
bcam generalizability, the RGB stream is deliberately
utilized, explicitly ignoring depth and infrared modal-
ities. The dataset is partitioned via a class-stratified

Signer Distribution and Identity Protocol:Data was col-

each volunteer performed any given gloss at most once.
Consequently, for every gloss class, the set of signers
present in the training partition is strictly disjoint from
the set present in the test partition guaranteeing a zero-
shot identity scenario for each word. While the total
count of unique signers across all classes was not formally
tracked due to the distributed, multi-year nature of col-
lection, the scale and diversity of acquisition sites make
cross-partition identity overlap across classes negligibly
unlikely. This protocol forces the architecture to learn
invariant kinematic semantics rather than memorizing
subject-specific anatomy.

4.3 Training Details and Reproducibility
Experiments utilized an Intel i9-13900K CPU and a
24GB RTX 4090 GPU. While temporal subsampling
used grayscale proxies, RTMPose skeletal extraction
utilized full-resolution RGB for maximum landmark
precision. The network trained for 100 epochs using
AdamW (with high weight decay to regularize the trans-
former) and a ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler. Strict
deterministic behavior ( seed = 42) across splits and
augmentations guaranteed exact reproducibility.
From the 7,527 training samples, an internal class-
stratified 80/20 split yields approximately 6,022 samples
for gradient updates and 1,505 samples for an internal
validation subset, used exclusively for checkpoint se-
lection via minimum validation loss monitoring. All
reported performance metrics are derived strictly from
the separate, held-out test repository of 1,926 samples,
which is never used during training or model selection.
All exhaustive configuration parameters including spa-
tial dimensions, ArcFace margins, and learning rates are
consolidated inAppendix A.

4.4 Evaluation on Public Benchmarks
To validate cross-domain and signer-independent gen-
eralization, our framework is evaluated on the Argen-
tinian Sign Language [ 47] LSA64, INCLUDE50, and
INCLUDE ISL benchmarks. For LSA64, a strict zero-
identity-leakage protocol (Subjects 1–8 for training, 9–10
for testing) is applied.
Our SVD sampler demonstrated robust efficiency
across framerates: on LSA64 (60 FPS), it condensed
127.8-frame sequences to 38.8 keyframes (69.6% reduc-
tion); on INCLUDE configurations (25 FPS), 64.25-
frame sequences were compressed to 26.7 keyframes
(58.4% reduction). This yields substantial computational
savings during pose extraction while preserving criti-
cal kinematics. As detailed inTable 4, our approach
achieves competitive predictive performance on both
datasets, proving our architecture successfully transfers
generalized representations to unseen signers and diverse
recording environments.
4.5 Comparison with Baseline Architectures
To isolate our architectural contributions, standard skele-
ton baselines (ST-GCN, CTR-GCN) are evaluated on
our 120-class benchmark using the exact same SVD-
extracted spatio-kinematic features.
As detailed inT able 5, our model significantly out-
performs both baselines. The sharp performance drop
in the standard networks (e.g., CTR-GCN dropping to
64.8% Test Acc) highlights a fundamental architectural
limitation: standard temporal graph networks implicitly
assume uniform time steps and fail to interpret irregu-
lar temporal gaps. By replacing standard convolutions
with a distance-aware Elastic Transformer and contin-
uous positional encodings, the proposed architecture
natively preserves physical signing rhythm, maximizing
the utility of subsampled data.
Furthermore,T able 6presents a targeted compar-
ison against highly parameterized vision architectures
(VideoMAE, MViTv2). Even against these resource-
intensive models, our lightweight framework demon-
strates highly competitive performance.
Ablation StudyTo quantify the impact of our architec-
tural contributions, an exhaustive ablation study (T able
7) is conducted. Removing the Continuous Positional
Encoding (PE) and Kinematic features causes a severe
performance collapse (dropping to 80.3% Test Acc),
proving that standard sequence modeling fails on irreg-
ular temporal gaps without explicit spatial-temporal
priors. The ablation also directly confirms the benefit of
entropy-driven selection over uniform sampling (87.5%
vs. 89.8%), validating that content-aware keyframe ex-
traction is essential. Furthermore, bypassing the SVD
Sampler to process “All Frames” yields only a marginal
1.2% accuracy gain, validating that our dimensional-
ity reduction pipeline successfully discards redundancy
while fully preserving critical gestural semantics.

5 Conclusion
This work presented an efficient hybrid framework for
large-vocabulary Indian Sign Language recognition. By
unifying spatial graph convolutions with elastic temporal
attention, the architecture effectively isolates invariant
kinematic structures from raw video sequences. A cor-
nerstone of this success is the dimensionality reduction
pipeline; the entropy-driven temporal subsampling dy-
namically filters out redundant frames, significantly re-
ducing computational overhead while preserving crucial
gestural semantics. Combined with ArcFace optimiza-
tion to enforce robust decision boundaries, the model

occlusions or extreme motion blur can corrupt initial
skeletal tokens and propagate errors through the net-
work. Additionally, the primary dataset’s acquisition
at 15 FPS may restrict the recognition of rapid finger-
spelling, despite the temporal convolution network’s
ability to capture local micro-motions. Furthermore,
the current structural embedding focuses strictly on
